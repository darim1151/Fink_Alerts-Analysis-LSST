## Headline findings

1. **The Fink CATS classifier labels almost everything "SN-like".** Against external labels from Fink's own crossmatches,
   spectroscopic TNS supernovae get SN-like 96% of the time once past the first alert (174/182). But 52% of VSX eclipsing
   binaries, 54% of pulsating stars and 66% of SIMBAD QSO/AGN are also called SN-like. Recall of the correct class is
   2–5% for periodic stars and AGN overall, rising to only 20–45% for objects with ≥10 detections. No CV/nova is called
   Fast (0/195) and no SLSN/TDE is called Long (0/12). SN-like is a default class, not a pure one. (K1, tables K1–K3.)
2. **SuperNNova SN-vs-others barely separates SNe from variables and AGN:** AUC 0.563 [0.514, 0.613], and 0.546 after
   removing first alerts (221 TNS SNe vs 43,834 reference non-SNe). (K2.)
3. **Labels are unstable.** 34% of consecutive delivered detections of the same object change CATS class (958,226 of
   2,824,189 pairs). The "first alert" state (−1, `is_first`) repeats: 218,354 objects carry it on two or more alerts,
   and 27% of first alerts are followed by another first alert. For objects with ≥20 detections only ~70–75% of
   intermediate labels already equal the final one. (L1.)
4. **The colour sample is dominated by transients on galaxies; the M-dwarf branch is stars.** Of 5,552 colour-plotted
   objects with Legacy DR8 morphology, 4,603 are extended and 949 point-like. The point-like fraction falls from ~95% at
   `r_tmpl ≈ 17` to < 5% beyond 21. The red branch (`g−r > 1.2`, `r−i > 0.6`) is 94% point-like (455 of 483 with morphology): M dwarfs,
   most carrying SN-like labels. (C1.)
5. **Alerting stars form a clean main sequence and a white-dwarf sequence.** 3,305 objects with Gaia DR3 parallax
   S/N > 5 trace the main sequence in `M_i,tmpl` vs `(r−i)_tmpl`, plus a separate group at `M_i ≈ 12–15`, `r−i ≈ 0`
   consistent with white dwarfs or CVs. Bulge and plane objects sit redder and fainter, as expected from uncorrected
   reddening. (D1.)
6. **Light Static rarely samples the peak.** For 79 TNS-typed transients with spectroscopic redshift, the brightest
   delivered absolute magnitude of the 41 SNe Ia spans −15.1 to −19.7, and 90% are fainter than the typical −19.3 peak. With host
   photo-z (37,336 objects), the faint envelope rises with redshift: that is the detection limit (Malmquist bias), not
   physics. (D2.)
7. **The alert stream has a reliability floor of 0.5.** Median reliability is 0.67 (i band lowest, u and y highest).
   Requiring median reliability ≥ 0.9 keeps 1,859 of 8,196 colour objects and removes the M-dwarf branch, so stellar-flare
   alerts are systematically lower-reliability. (Q1.)
8. **SSO is consistent with asteroids.** Outside bulge and plane fields the SSO share of alerts is 10–22% at |β| < 14°,
   0.5–1.3% at 18–24° and ≈ 0 beyond 40°. (O1.)
9. **The data are several distinct programmes.** Low-dust WFD (3.05M alerts, 70% first alerts), Galactic bulge (1.37M)
   and plane (0.79M), deep-drilling fields (1.51M; richest class mix, median reliability 0.82) and the M49 field (0.34M).
   i and z dominate because of `pairs_iz` visits (2.85M alerts). Legacy DR8 morphology and photo-z exist only outside the
   bulge and plane. Population studies should be split by field first. (S1–S3, T1, T2.)

## Methods

- **Inputs.** Catalog opened read-only with `open_catalog` (raw stat fingerprints re-verified; catalog SHA256 `bb308e31…cefb`
  unchanged). Only its pinned 71,042 Parquet files were read, with pyarrow. Populations follow `analysis_contract_v1`
  (DIA: `is_sso` false and positive `diaObjectId`; SSO: `is_sso` true and zero/null id); counts are asserted against the
  catalog's `source_qc`. Template magnitudes come from the audited colour run (`alert-colour-diagrams-20261008-v1`).
- **Object attributes.** Broker labels and scores come from each DIA object's latest delivered alert (max time, then
  max `diaSourceId`). Crossmatch fields take the latest non-null, non-`Fail` value. Field is the most frequent programme.
- **Programme groups** from `target_name`, in precedence order: any `ddf_*` → deep-drilling; `field_m49`; `neutrino*` → ToO;
  `alt:` → engineering; `dusty_plane` → plane; `bulgy` → bulge; `nes`; `lowdust` → WFD.
- **External labels** (precedence TNS > VSX > SIMBAD): TNS type from `tns_type_recomputed`, falling back to `xm.tns_type`
  (SN* → SN, SLSN*/TDE* → SLSN/TDE). VSX types are grouped by their first token into eclipsing, pulsating, rotational,
  CV/nova and other. SIMBAD otypes are grouped into QSO/AGN, galaxy and star. The expected CATS classes are SN → 11,
  SLSN/TDE → 13, CV → 12, eclipsing/pulsating/rotational → 21, QSO/AGN → 22. Intervals are 95% Wilson. AUC uses the
  rank formula with 1,000 bootstrap resamples (seed 20261008).
- **Distances.** `M_i,tmpl = i_tmpl + 5 log10(ϖ/mas) − 10` for Gaia parallax S/N > 5. Peak magnitudes are the brightest
  positive delivered `psfFlux` (S/N ≥ 5). Distance modulus uses flat ΛCDM with Planck18 H0 and Ωm, integrated numerically
  (radiation neglected, < 0.01 mag for z < 1.5). Host photo-z uses Legacy DR8 objects with pstar < 0.5 and 0.02 < z < 1.5.
- **Variability.** Fink SNAD `lc_features[i]` at the latest i-band alert, computed by Fink on the difference flux over the
  alert's previous-detection history; amplitude is `standard_deviation / median templateFlux`. Skewness is in flux
  (negative = dimming), the opposite sign of Sesar's magnitude skewness.
- **Independent cross-check** (catalog SQL and brute force, not the suite code): nightly totals match `daily_coverage`
  on all 139 dates; field-table totals reproduce 7,101,947 / 6,311,364 / 790,583; transition pairs equal DIA sources minus
  DIA objects (2,824,189); VSX-labelled objects agree to 3 (TNS precedence); the Gaia sample (3,305) and AUC (0.5628) are
  reproduced exactly.

## Limitations

Crossmatches are Fink's positional matches with no separation delivered (except Mangrove), so chance matches are
possible in crowded fields. External labels are themselves incomplete and biased toward bright, known objects. There is
no extinction or K-correction, and photometric calibration is not independently validated. Detection index counts
detections inside this cohort; Fink's classifiers also see earlier Rubin history. Amplitudes use detected epochs only and
are biased high near the limit. There are no pixel flags, so saturated or blended templates cannot be removed. Zero-delivery
dates are not zero Rubin alerts. No raw, manifest, catalog or cohort file was written; no Fink, Kafka or API query was made.
